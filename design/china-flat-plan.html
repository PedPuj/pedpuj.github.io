中国The China flat plan

It is well organised. It is not yet well composed.

The journey, the chapters, the paper that shifts city by city, one photograph at a time — that structure is right, and it is better than most photographers' sites. What is missing is pacing. Of the 32 pages, 23 are one photograph at exactly the same height in exactly the same place — and 18 of those are the identical 1053 × 702 rectangle.

The one cause

Every size on the page comes from a single number

A cap on the photograph's height — and that cap takes only four values: 78vh for a normal frame, 62vh if it opens a city, 56vh for a pair, 46vh for a pair that opens a city. Width is whatever the aspect ratio then makes it.

So the shape of every page is decided by the proportions of the photograph and nothing else. In a book the editor decides how big a picture is. Here the camera does. Everything below follows from that.

Six things, worst first

What is wrong

01

Every photograph is the same height, so every upright is a small picture

landscape  1053 × 702 px  =  739,000 px²
upright    468 × 702 px  =  328,000 px²  (44%)

Both are capped at 78vh, so the upright loses more than half the area — and it loses it on every one of the fourteen uprights in the edit. The photograph you close the whole series on, 037, the monk in the last of the light, is one of the smallest pictures on the site.

A book would never do this. A book gives an upright the height of the page and a landscape the width of it.

02
Your call, not the code's

Beijing ends with four identical rectangles, and Xi'an opens with two more

009 010 011 012  four landscapes, 1053 × 702, consecutive
013 014          two more, straight after the chapter break
025 026 · 029 030  Guilin does it again

Six near-identical shapes in a row, right in the middle of the book. The chapter line between 012 and 013 is the only thing that tells you anything changed.

Nothing in the code causes this — it is where the pairs and the uprights happen to fall in the edit. Moving one upright into that run fixes it.

03

The chapter rule is wider than the photograph it introduces

rule    1312 px — the full measure, every time
under it  837 px  Beijing, Xi'an, Shanghai
          600 px  Hangzhou
          372 px  Guilin — the type is 3.5× the picture

At Guilin the line stops reading as a caption for the photograph and starts reading as furniture the photograph is sitting underneath. The rule was given the full measure so that "the two ends of the line hold the width the photographs sit inside" — but the photographs never sit inside it. They are height-capped, so they almost never reach it.

04

The first photograph of every city is the smallest one in it

a normal frame  78vh  702 px tall
one that opens a city  62vh  558 px tall  (−21%)

Arriving somewhere new gives you a smaller picture than the page before it. That is backwards, and it is the price the layout pays to fit the chapter line onto the same screen. The height is being taken from the photograph to pay for the type.

05

Hangzhou opens on the two smallest pictures in the book

031 + 032  276 × 414 px each
together    576 px of a 1312 px measure — 44%
above them  a 1312 px rule

A pair that also opens a city gets 46vh, the smallest cap in the system — two penalties stacked on the one page you meant to give emphasis to. It is the weakest page in the sequence.

06
Your call, not the code's

Shanghai is two frames with a chapter heading on it

Beijing 12 · Xi'an 11 · Guilin 7 · Hangzhou 5 · Shanghai 2

The last city gets a rule, its name in two scripts and "036 – 037 · 2 FRAMES" — and then the book stops. The journey doesn't close, it runs out. Either Shanghai earns five or six frames, or it isn't a chapter: let the two photographs close the series without a heading and let Hangzhou be the last city.

What I would do

Three moves

No work for you

1 · Let the orientation set the height

One rule instead of one number: an upright is capped at 88vh, a landscape stays at 78vh, and a pair of two uprights gets 72vh rather than 56. Openings come up from 62vh to 70vh, and an opening that is a pair stops being punished twice.

It cannot equalise them — a 16:9 screen will always favour a landscape — but it closes most of the gap, and it makes the page vary without anyone deciding anything.

No work for you

2 · Let the rule follow the picture

The chapter rule takes the width of the photograph beneath it rather than the full measure. The city's name then sits on the picture's left edge and the frame range on its right — the line becomes a caption belonging to that photograph instead of a bar across the page. It is the second half of fault 03, shown above.

One word, on a few lines

3 · Give yourself a size word

A size field on a frame, exactly like pair — left out on almost every line, and set to 'full' or 'quiet' on the handful that earn it. full runs a photograph to 92vh, near the full measure; quiet holds one back to 58vh.

This is the thing that was set aside as "asymmetric editorial grid — needs a decision per photograph". It doesn't. It needs a decision on six or seven photographs per series, and the rest inherit. Six loud frames in 37 is what makes a book feel edited rather than generated.

What is already right, and worth not breaking: the restraint, the paper, the mono, nothing ever drawn on top of a photograph, the five words down the margin, the counter that names the city. Those are the parts a photographer's site usually gets wrong, and they are the reason the faults above are worth fixing at all — the frame is good enough that the pacing is now the thing you can see.

Moves 1 and 2 are code, and I can do them now. Faults 02 and 06 and the choice of which photographs get full are yours.
